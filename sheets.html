Your bank SMS, in your own Google Sheet and on your home screen

Every bank payment you get an SMS for shows up on your home screen and in a Google Sheet you own, sorted into categories. Free. No bank login.

Your iPhone → Your Google Sheet → Home screen
  • Everything stays in your Google account. Nobody else can see it, including the person who made this.
  • The script can open only this one spreadsheet. It has no access to your Gmail, Drive or other files.
  • OTP messages are processed on your phone and never sent anywhere.
  • All the code is public: read it on GitHub.

Before you start

  • iOS 26 or later.
  • Only one Google account signed in, and it's a personal one (not work or school).
  • About 5 minutes, all on your phone.

Set it up

  1. Make your copy

    Google asks Make a copy? Tap it. Your copy opens in Safari.

    Make my copy

  2. Switch it on

    In your copy, in Safari:

    1. Tap aA (or the ☰ icon left of docs.google.com) → Request Desktop Website.
    2. Extensions → Apps Script.
    3. Deploy → New deployment. Check that Who has access says Anyone, then tap Deploy.
      “Anyone” only means your shortcut can reach your Sheet without signing in. Nobody can read anything without your phone's connection code.
    4. Authorize access → your account → Continue.
    5. Copy the Web app URL.

  3. Add the two shortcuts

    Tap each button. Safari downloads the file: tap the downloads icon in the address bar, tap the file, then Add Shortcut.

    Add “Spend Tracker” Add “Spend Tracker Auto”

    Then switch on its SMS triggers, which your iPhone adds switched off: in Shortcuts, tap ⋯ on Spend Tracker Auto. On each of the 3 When I receive a message blocks, tap where › and turn on Automation.

    On iOS 26, skip “Spend Tracker Auto” and make the 3 automations by hand instead, each one running Spend Tracker: see how.

    “Spend Tracker Auto” runs Spend Tracker when a bank SMS arrives. Reinstalling? Delete the old ones first, or the SMS triggers get lost.

  4. Connect

    Open Shortcuts and tap Spend Tracker to run it (or open it and tap ▶). Paste the Web app URL and allow what it asks. You'll see Connected ✅.

Done. Your next bank SMS appears in your Sheet within about a minute.

Using it

  • Categories: pick one in the Transactions tab. It's remembered for that payee from then on.
  • Cash: tap Spend Tracker any time and enter the amount.
  • Same small payment every morning, like auto fares? The Sheet asks you once. You can also add your own rule in the Rules tab.
  • Summary and Analytics show each month and each category.
  • Home-screen widget with a daily spending graph (optional): set it up.

Questions

Which banks work?

Debit and card SMS from most Indian banks, including HDFC, SBI, ICICI, Axis, Kotak, Union Bank, PNB, Bank of Baroda, Canara and IDFC FIRST. If a message isn't counted, it appears in the Skipped tab with the reason.

Does it read all my messages?

No. It runs only on messages containing “debit”, “spent” or “sent Rs”. OTP messages are dropped on your phone. Anything that isn't a bank payment is ignored and not saved.

Starting over with a new copy?

Your phone remembers the Sheet it's connected to. In the Setup tab of the old Sheet, type yes next to “New phone?”. Then tap Spend Tracker: it says it's no longer connected and asks for the new Web app URL.

New phone?

In the Setup tab of your Sheet, type yes next to “New phone?”. Then add the shortcuts on the new phone and run Spend Tracker once.

How do I stop it or remove it?

Delete the two shortcuts. To remove the script's access, go to myaccount.google.com/permissions → Spend Tracker → Remove access. Deleting the Sheet deletes everything.

Not yet.

Does it cost anything?

No, it's free.
